## Regression Decision

Response success means a checked successful Result in the original HTTP response,
divided by sent requests. Success p99 starts at the planned arrival and includes
generator delay. It contains successful responses only; follow-up observations do
not enter it. A loss greater than five percentage points, or p99 growth greater
than 20% at success rates within five points, is one regressed pair. Two pairs
reject a candidate. These were the predeclared rules, not fitted to these results.

| Case | Pair 1: B − A success pp / p99 ratio | Pair 2 | Pair 3 | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| any-100 | +0.00 / 1.003× | +0.00 / 1.006× | +0.00 / 1.008× | no_detected_regression |
| any-500 | +12.37 / 1.094× | -12.60 / 0.894× | +0.93 / 1.181× | no_detected_regression |
| any-1000 | +3.93 / 0.937× | -5.40 / 0.662× | -0.44 / 0.648× | no_detected_regression |
| any-2000 | generator limited | generator limited | generator limited | inconclusive |
| targeted-500 | -7.16 / 1.423× | +1.97 / 1.248× | -10.96 / 1.982× | regressed |
| mixed-500 | +0.21 / 0.927× | -5.02 / 0.394× | +1.20 / 0.399× | no_detected_regression |

For `targeted-500`, pair 1 lost 7.16 points, pair 2 grew p99 by 24.80%
with a 1.97-point success improvement, and pair 3 lost 10.96 points. All three
were comparable and regressed. The first and third p99 increases were 42.34%
and 98.20%, respectively. This is sufficient to withdraw the candidate; this
experiment does not isolate the internal cause of its tail behavior.

All six `any-2000` runs were generator limited: 11,312–15,693 requests per run
were not sent, with schedule-lag p99 of 117.84–142.92ms. They remain in the report
but establish neither server capacity nor candidate regression. Other cases had
no not-sent, unknown-submission or protocol-error samples. Single-pair regressions
in other cases do not meet the two-pair rule and do not establish equivalence or speedup.

## Next Evidence To Collect

1. **Dispatch and result timing:** the non-limited 100/s cases already have about
   305ms successful p99, while append command cost changes barely move it.
   DEFAULT assignment interval is 100ms and dispatch is bounded to 100 Items per
   Task round. Record queue residence and eligible-round timing before proposing
   a cadence change; these constants alone do not prove a 1,000/s capacity limit.
2. **Targeted lease reuse:** targeted-500 triggered the repeatable rejection while
   all its accepted Items eventually succeeded. Correlate per-target availability,
   assignment, command publication and result consumption timing through their
   existing owners. The current aggregates do not identify which stage caused it.
3. **HTTP/result observation:** configured 1s wait is not an end-to-end deadline.
   Task Call performs admission, submission and initial observation before creating
   its deferred waiter; the result probe separately batches up to 256 Items with
   50/100/250ms age-based intervals. Capture time in these stages and timeout
   delivery before changing result observation policy. Mixed-load backlog shows
   why eventual-success percentages alone are insufficient.
4. **Load-generator boundary:** 2,000/s repeatedly saturated the fixed 4,096
   in-flight budget. Keep those rows classified as limited. A future capacity
   claim needs an explicitly revised, versioned generator/fixture and independent
   evidence, not an assertion that this run found server capacity.

Long-running soak, cross-machine networks, more active Tasks/Groups, fairness and
new call semantics remain unproved. The platform is not restricted to RPC; this
lane establishes one online-call workload and its coexistence with background work.
